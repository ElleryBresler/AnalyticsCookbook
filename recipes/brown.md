# Bolognese Sauce

**Contributed by:** Eric Brown
**Serves:** 6  |  **Time:** 120 minutes

## Why this one

I found this recipe a handful of years ago and it has been my go-to ever since.  I especially like making it when I am entertaining guests because it is easy to prepare ahead of time, makes the house smell great when it simmers, and has always been a crowd pleaser.

## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 onion, chopped
- 1 carrot, peeled and finely chopped
- 1 stalk celery, finely chopped
- 1 lb ground beef
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1/2 cup dry white wine
- 1 (15-oz.) can crushed tomatoes
- 3 tbsp tomato paste
- 2 cups low-sodium beef or chicken broth
- 1 bay leaf
- 1 cup whole milk (or heavy cream)
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt
- Freshly ground black pepper

## Steps

1. Heat the olive oil in a large Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook until softened, about 7 minutes.

2. Add the ground beef and garlic. Break up the beef while it cooks and continue cooking until the meat is no longer pink and is lightly browned, about 8 minutes.

3. Add the white wine and bring to a simmer, scraping the bottom of the pan to loosen any browned bits.  Cook until the wine has mostly reduced.

4. Stir in the crushed tomatoes, tomato paste, broth, and bay leaf. Reduce the heat and simmer for about 1 hour.

5. Remove the bay leaf and gradually stir in the milk. Cover with the lid slightly ajar and continue simmering, stirring occasionally, until the milk is incorporated and the sauce is rich and silky, about 45 minutes.

6. Season with kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper to taste. Serve over your preferred pasta.

## Notes

## Notes

A wider pasta such as tagliatelle or pappardelle works especially well with a thick sauce like Bolognese. Give the sauce plenty of time to simmer—the long cooking time helps develop the flavor and texture. Freshly grated Parmesan can be added when serving.

The original recipe calls for whole milk, which gives the sauce a traditional creamy texture without making it overly rich. Heavy cream can be substituted for a richer, thicker sauce. If using heavy cream, consider starting with less than the full cup and adding more until the sauce reaches your preferred consistency.
